Meet

NymphCount

The tray counter that shows its work.

NymphCount counts and identifies benthic macroinvertebrates from a photo of your sorting tray, then computes the water-quality indices — HBI and %EPT — from the tally. You stay the taxonomist; it does the counting and the arithmetic.

The App

A photo goes in. Indices come out.

Sort the sample the way you already do, photograph the tray, and NymphCount takes it from there — every specimen marked on the photo, every mark open to your inspection and correction.

◎

Count

Every specimen it finds gets a mark on the photo and a place in the total — one mark per animal, placed where the animal is, so the count can be checked at a glance.

≋

Group & identify

Counted specimens are sorted into the kinds the indices are built from. Anything that can’t be settled is set aside as unsorted rather than guessed into a group.

⚖

Compute the indices

HBI, %EPT, and EPT richness are computed from the verified tally, with the full tally sheet laid out — every group, its members, and the totals.

The letter system

Every kind has a letter. Every letter has a colour.

Every animal on the tray wears two marks: its number in the count, and its group’s letter in its group’s colour — the same letters and colours the app stamps on the photo itself. These are the seventeen groups the water-quality indices are built from, A through Q. What can’t be settled honestly wears the grey 0, Unsorted, until you call it.

AMayflies BStoneflies CCaddisflies DTrue flies & midges EScuds FIsopods GCrayfish HSnails IClams & mussels JLeeches KAquatic worms LDragonflies & damselflies MBeetles NHellgrammites & alderflies OTrue bugs PFlatworms QMites 0Unsorted

A · B · C The clean-water core. Mayflies, stoneflies, and caddisflies are the EPT groups — the kinds most sensitive to pollution, and the ones %EPT and EPT richness are counted from. When a stream is healthy, its tray leans on these three letters.

Who it’s for

Built for the people at the bench

Anyone who has spent an afternoon hunched over a sorting tray with a tally counter knows the bottleneck isn’t the science — it’s the counting.

⌂

Teaching & research labs

Bench counts for biomonitoring work, with the tally and the indices in the shapes your analysis already uses — and the raw count kept beside every correction, so the record stays honest.

✎

Students

Learn the groups while you count. Specimens are sorted into the kinds the indices are built from, and the ones that need a closer look are flagged rather than silently named.

≋

Field researchers & watershed groups

Volunteer and field biomonitoring runs on trays counted away from the lab. NymphCount works fully offline, on the Mac already in the field station.

Status

Checked first, claimed later

NymphCount is in active development and bench testing. It is being checked against hand-counted trays before it is offered to anyone — and no accuracy figure will appear on this site until that checking is done and the number is earned.
